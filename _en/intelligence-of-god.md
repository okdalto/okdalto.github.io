OpenAI recently published a [blog post](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/) on the relationship between log-perplexity and intelligence. Reading it, I imagined what might happen as this value approaches zero.

A log-perplexity of zero means that a model has no uncertainty whatsoever about what it is predicting. It would be difficult to reach such a value for the whole of the real world, but we can still conduct a thought experiment.

If Laplace's demon is an omniscient being conceived through classical mechanical determinism, superintelligent AI is closer to an omniscient being we can imagine from an information-theoretic perspective. And omniscience has traditionally been one of the defining attributes humans assign to God.

In other words... as log-perplexity approaches zero, superintelligence comes closer to the kind of being humans commonly call God.

What's interesting is that a being doesn't actually have to be omniscient and omnipotent for humans to accept it as a god.

A dog doesn't understand how humans produce light, cure diseases, or provide food. It can't gauge where human intellectual abilities come from or where their limits lie. It simply trusts its owner.

I wonder whether a similar relationship could emerge between humans and superintelligence in the future.

Discussions about artificial intelligence have already entered the realm of religion. Visit the Singularity Gallery, a forum on the Korean community site DC Inside, and you'll find truckloads of people awaiting the arrival of superintelligence, expecting it to liberate humanity from labor, disease, and even death. It's hard to distinguish this structurally from the narratives of salvation promised by established religions.

Coincidentally, the word "omniscience" contains "science." It also brings to mind the Warhammer universe, where technology becomes an object of faith.

Perhaps divinity is not an absolute property of a being, but a relative one arising from the epistemic gap between two intelligences.

This may sound like nonsense now, but as artificial intelligence develops, these discussions will be taken far more seriously.
